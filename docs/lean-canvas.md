# SyndicateLend — Lean Canvas

| | |
|---|---|
| **Problem** | 1. Loan assignments settle in the mid-to-high teens of business days (LSTA 2025) because ownership, eligibility, documents and cash sit in separate systems. 2. Each party reconciles positions and interest independently; breaks trigger delayed-compensation claims. 3. Controls (KYC, transfer restrictions, internal approvals) are applied outside the transfer itself. *Existing alternatives:* ClearPar for settlement workflow, Loan IQ for agent books, Versana for agent data, and tokenised private-credit wrappers that do not touch the assignment. |
| **Customer segments** | Administrative and facility agents (the register owners); CLO managers and credit funds with active secondary trading; loan-operations teams that bear the cost of delay. *Early adopters:* one agency desk plus one buy-side fund on a single facility. In India: private-sector banks that act as lead bank for consortium and syndicated facilities and have indicated interest (ICICI Bank, HDFC Bank, SBI). |
| **Unique value proposition** | A loan trade settles as one controlled transaction: the assignment and the cash move together, eligibility is re-checked at execution, institutional approvals are enforced in the wallet, and interest is computed from confidential terms with a public proof. *High-level concept:* DvP for loan assignments, run on the agent's register. |
| **Solution** | ATS security per tranche with eligibility controls; RFQ market on HCS; Privy 2-of-3 quorum desk wallets; `SettlementEngine` atomic DvP scheduled by the Hedera Schedule Service; Chainlink CRE confidential accrual with salted HCS commitments; batched HTS interest payout. |
| **Channels** | Fullmetal Finance's existing relationships with institutional OTC derivatives users (collateral and settlement desks overlap with loan operations); the Secondary Loan Market Association (India) and LSTA operations working groups; agent-bank technology partnerships (integration with Loan IQ, ClearPar, Versana feeds rather than replacement). |
| **Revenue streams** | Settlement fee per completed trade; facility onboarding and integration fee; annual register-maintenance fee per facility; interest-distribution service fee. Hypotheses, not validated prices. |
| **Cost structure** | Solo founder plus one integration engineer for the pilot; private Hedera deployment (HashSphere) and node costs; legal opinion per pilot facility; Privy and Chainlink usage; compliance and audit tooling. |
| **Key metrics** | Agreement-to-settlement-ready time; unmatched exceptions and their age; reconciliation hours per facility per month; failed settlements by cause; delayed-compensation events avoided; shadow-register agreement rate against the agent's books. |
| **Unfair advantage** | Founder's four years building institutional OTC settlement and collateral infrastructure, an existing derivatives product to cross-sell from, and a design that follows the agent-register legal model rather than a fund-token wrapper, which is what makes agents willing to run it as a shadow register. |
